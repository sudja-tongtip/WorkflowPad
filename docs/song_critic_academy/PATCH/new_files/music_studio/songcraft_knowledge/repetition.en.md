## What It Is
Repetition is bringing a word, line, image or section back again. Contrast is what changes when it comes back: a word, the context, or a listener who now knows more. Nearly every song uses repetition. The question is not whether there is "too much or too little", but what each return does.

What must be kept apart is intention and effect: a repetition that is meant is not therefore effective, and a lot of repetition is not therefore bad. Look at what the listener gets when it returns.

Berklee's course that starts from the title has lessons on developing the title motivically and on contrasting sections [R1]. The six functions of repetition below are our own synthesis.

## Why It Works
- Repetition builds familiarity, and when the context changes, the same line gains a new meaning.
- Repetition builds a mood, such as being stuck, going round, or waiting.
- A Chorus repeated word for word can weigh more when the Verses between its returns develop, and can go flat when they do not.
- Contrast between sections tells the listener they have reached a new point, which gives the repetition of other sections more value.

## How to Use
When you see a line or section that repeats, ask three things: (1) why does it repeat; (2) what does the listener get when it returns; (3) what has changed (a word, the context, or what the listener knows). If you cannot answer 2 and 3, ask whether the intention actually works.

### emphasis | Repeat to stress the meaning
- Hammers home what the song wants remembered. It works when each return adds weight.

### familiarity | Repeat to build familiarity
- Lets the listener sing along or wait for the return. Take care that it does not become recitation.

### atmosphere | Repeat to build a mood
- The return of the same image or sound creates stillness or circling. It works when small details change so that time can move.

### recontext | Repeat to change meaning through context
- The same line returns after a new event and so means something else.

### stuck | Repeat to show being stuck in a feeling
- Use when the singer circles one thought. Check that the listener feels the being stuck and not that the writer ran out of ideas.

### contrast | Make a contrast between sections
- A section that differs (in wording, view, length) gives the repeating section somewhere to stand and lets the listener feel change [R1].

## Examples
The lines below were written for teaching.

- Word for word, same context: the Chorus "รอเธออยู่ตรงนี้ ที่เดิมทุกวัน" (waiting for you here, the same place every day) three times, with nothing changing in the Verses between → the listener knew at the first time; the third gives nothing new
- Word for word, new context: Verse 1 rain falls and she has not come; Verse 2 she sends a message saying she is busy; the same Chorus second time now sounds like a hope beginning to shake
- Repeating for mood, and it works: "ฝนเคาะหลังคาเบา ๆ" (rain taps lightly on the roof) in every Chorus while the Verses move "curtain moves → shadow grows longer → nobody opens the door"; the repeated image lets time be felt even though the room does not change
- Repeating for mood, and it does not work: the image repeats and the Verses also repeat the same images with nothing moving → the stillness has not been made to mean anything

Comparison question: what does the listener know more when the line comes back? "It was meant to repeat" does not answer that. The effect on the listener does.

## Common Pitfalls
- Telling yourself that much repetition is always bad, or that deliberate repetition is always good. Both can be wrong.
- Using "I meant it" as the reason without checking what happens to the listener.
- Repeating a whole section while nothing in between changes.
- Repeating words that are not the idea (repeating what the listener does not need to remember).
- Changing words in a repeat for no reason, so the listener cannot tell why they changed.

## Try It Yourself
(1) List the lines or sections that repeat in your song. (2) Beside each, write "repeats in order to ___" and "the second/third time the listener gets ___ more". (3) If you cannot fill in the second, try three options: change one word, change the section between the repeats, or remove one repeat. (4) Read the whole song again and see how the effect differs. You may decide to keep the original repetition if you can see why it works.
