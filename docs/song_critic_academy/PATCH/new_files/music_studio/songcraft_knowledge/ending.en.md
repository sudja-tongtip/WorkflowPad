## What It Is
The ending is what the listener carries away when the song stops. "Payoff" means that the ending answers what the song set up: a direct answer, a change in the meaning of what was set up, or a deliberate leaving open that means something. A song does not have to answer every question it opens.

The six forms below are categories we made for learning. They are not a theory with anyone's endorsement [R1], and among the references we checked there is none we can cite for teaching this classification of endings.

## Why It Works
- Listeners remember endings. An ending decides what the whole song seems to mean when looked back on.
- Responding to what was set up (an image, a question, a contradiction) makes the song feel complete, even if the response is not a direct answer.
- Leaving it open works when the listener knows what was left open and why, not when the song seems to have forgotten it.
- Returning to the opening image has power when its meaning has changed by then.

## How to Use
Before choosing an ending, look back at what the opening promised (an image, a question, a contradiction) and ask whether the ending will answer it, change its meaning, or leave it open on purpose, and how the listener will know it was on purpose.

### resolution | Resolution
- The problem or question gets an answer. Clear and satisfying, but it can feel too easy if a lot of tension was built.

### open | Open Ending: left open in a meaningful way
- It does not answer, but it makes the question weigh more. It works when what is left open is the song's own subject.

### circular | Circular Ending
- Goes back to the opening image or line. It has power when the context has changed so that it means something different; if not, it may feel like plain repetition.

### closure | Emotional Closure
- The story is not settled, but the feeling arrives somewhere, such as acceptance, exhaustion or forgiveness.

### recontext | Recontextualization
- The old thing returns with a new meaning, for example the umbrella that hung by the door becomes something ready to be used.

### reversal | Contrast / Reversal
- Turns the expectation over. It needs something laid down earlier; otherwise it is a free-floating trick.

## Examples
The lines below were written for teaching. The song opens with "ร่มสีเขียวยังแขวนอยู่หลังประตู ฉันไม่กล้าหยิบมัน" (the green umbrella still hangs behind the door; I do not dare take it).

- Resolution: "วันนี้ฝนตก ฉันหยิบร่มเดินออกไป / ไม่ได้ลืมเธอ แต่ฉันไม่หยุดอยู่ตรงนั้นอีกแล้ว" (it rains today, I take the umbrella and walk out; I have not forgotten her, but I do not stop there any more)
- Open ending: "ฝนยังตกอยู่ ฉันยืนมองประตู / มือยังไม่ได้ขยับ... แต่ฉันเริ่มนับว่ากี่ครั้งแล้ว" (it is still raining; I stand looking at the door; my hand has not moved... but I have started counting how many times) → no answer, but the listener sees the hesitation changing
- Circular: "ร่มสีเขียวยังแขวนอยู่หลังประตู" → the same line, but the listener now knows what it carries
- Emotional closure: "ฉันไม่รู้ว่าเธอจะกลับมาไหม / แต่คืนนี้ฉันนอนหลับได้" (I do not know whether you will come back, but tonight I can sleep) → nothing is revealed, but the feeling arrives
- Recontextualization: "ร่มสีเขียวหลังประตู / วันนี้มันเป็นของฉัน" (the green umbrella behind the door, today it is mine) → the same image, a new meaning
- Reversal: "ร่มสีเขียวหลังประตู / ฉันให้คนข้างบ้านยืมไปแล้ว" (the green umbrella behind the door; I have lent it to the neighbour) → turns the expectation; something must be set up earlier

A song that opens with the umbrella and ends on something else entirely (for example, the seasons in general) does not respond to what it opened, and listeners often feel something went missing.

## Common Pitfalls
- Believing every question that was opened has to be answered.
- Leaving it open because you do not know how to end, not because you meant to.
- A circular ending in which the context did not change.
- A safe or too wide ending that has nothing to do with what was set up (a general comforting line).
- A twist at the end with nothing laid down for it before.

## Try It Yourself
(1) Write what your opening promises the listener. (2) Write whether the ending answers it, changes its meaning, or leaves it open, and how. (3) If the ending is unrelated to the opening, write two endings in two of the six forms and compare them. (4) If you leave it open on purpose, ask what will tell the listener that this was your intention.
