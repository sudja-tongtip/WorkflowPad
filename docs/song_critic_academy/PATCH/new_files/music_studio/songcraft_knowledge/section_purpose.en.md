## What It Is
The sections of a song are more than names. Each has a job for the section before it and the one after it. The common names are Intro, Verse, Pre-Chorus, Chorus, Bridge and Outro, but songs do not have to share one structure. A good song may have no Bridge at all, or only two Verses.

Berklee's lyric-writing course lists lessons on verse development techniques, contrasting sections and song forms [R1], and Pat Pattison's book on lyric form and structure deals with the shape of lyrics, such as line length and rhyme placement [R2]. What we stress is "what does one section do for the next", more than memorising names.

## Why It Works
- Listeners feel a journey when each section adds something the one before did not have (a fact, a view, a feeling, tension).
- A Chorus takes its meaning from the Verse before it: when the Verses develop, each return of the Chorus can weigh more.
- Differences between sections (view, the rhythm of the wording, line length) tell the listener they have arrived somewhere new.
- When two sections do the same job over again, the song feels as if it circles in place, unless that is meant.

## How to Use
Go through the song one section at a time and write half a line: "this section adds ___ to the one before and prepares ___ for the next." Pay most attention to the links Verse 1 → Verse 2 → Chorus → Bridge.

### intro | Intro
- Sets a world or a mood and tells the listener where they have come in. Some songs have no sung Intro.

### verse | Verse
- Tells or shows the details, and should develop the idea: a Verse 2 that says the same thing in new words stalls the song.
- Ask what fact, view or consequence Verse 2 has that Verse 1 does not.

### pre-chorus | Pre-Chorus
- Builds tension or turns the direction so the Chorus has somewhere to land. Not every song needs one.

### chorus | Chorus
- Says the core idea or main feeling and is often the part that returns. Its value is whether each return comes with a new context.

### bridge | Bridge
- Brings in a new view or feeling and can make the last Chorus mean something different. If it only restates the old image, it may not be doing a Bridge's job.

### outro | Outro
- Closes the song: it may return to the opening image, shrink the idea, or leave it open. It should connect to what the song opened.

## Examples
The lines below were written for teaching: an imaginary song about "the shoes still by the door".

- Verse 1 (setting): "รองเท้าคู่เก่าของพ่อยังวางอยู่ข้างประตู / ฝุ่นจับที่ส้นมาเกือบปี" (Dad's old shoes still sit beside the door; dust has gathered on the heel for almost a year)
- A Verse 2 that adds a new fact: "แม่เก็บเสื้อของพ่อไปแล้วทุกตัว / แต่ไม่ยอมแตะรองเท้าคู่นั้นเลย" (Mum has put away all of Dad's shirts but will not touch those shoes) → we see how Mum feels, which Verse 1 did not say
- A Verse 2 that stands still: "รองเท้าของพ่อยังอยู่ตรงนั้น / ฉันมองมันทุกวันเมื่อผ่านไป" (Dad's shoes are still there; I look at them every day I pass) → it says again what Verse 1 said
- A Bridge that brings a new view: "วันหนึ่งน้องชายลองสวมมัน / ใหญ่เกินไปสำหรับเขา แต่เขาไม่ยอมถอด" (one day my little brother tried them on; too big for him, but he would not take them off) → the last Chorus about the shoes now also means who steps forward next

## Common Pitfalls
- Two sections doing the same job (for example Verse 2 repeating Verse 1) for no reason.
- Adding a Bridge only because a song "should have" one.
- Treating Verse-Chorus as a rule instead of asking what this song needs.
- A section that adds nothing is not always to be cut: it may be a place to breathe. But you should know what it does.
- Mixing up what a section does for the song with the label on the section.

## Try It Yourself
(1) List the sections of your song and finish one sentence for each: "this section adds ___ to the one before." (2) Mark the ones you cannot answer, or answer the same as the one before. (3) For each marked section, note three options: add a new fact, change the view, or change the section's job. (4) Read the Chorus before and after that section and see whether its meaning changes.
