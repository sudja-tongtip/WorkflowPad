## What It Is
Imagery and metaphor are ways of making a feeling or an idea tangible: through sensory detail, images, comparisons (metaphor) and symbols. Choosing between concrete and abstract language is also an artistic decision.

Pat Pattison teaches sense-bound writing, "object writing": choose one object and write about it through several senses [R1]. Berklee's course Lyric Writing: Tools and Strategies has lessons on object writing and on metaphor [R2], and Andrea Stolpe's book Beginning Songwriting has a part that deals with sensory writing [R3].

## Why It Works
- Details that come through the senses let the listener see or feel along faster than a stated emotion does.
- A specific image (a detail only this song has) is remembered and tells the listener what situation the singer is in.
- An image tied to the central idea works on two levels: as a detail and as meaning.
- Plain language can be strong if the right thing is chosen. Beautiful words do not always mean deep content.

## How to Use
Ask of each image "what does it do for the song", rather than "is it pretty", and check whether it connects with the idea of the song and its tone.

### sensory | Sensory detail
- Write about one thing with sound, smell, touch, taste and what the body feels, not only what is seen, then choose the detail that belongs to this song [R1].

### imagery | Imagery
- Pick an image that makes the situation clear at a glance, for example "two coffee cups" says there used to be two people.

### metaphor | Metaphor
- Compare one thing with another to show a new side. Check that the comparison stays consistent and does not mix into confusion.

### symbol | Symbolism
- An object or image that returns and carries more than itself, such as an umbrella that still hangs, meaning not yet letting go.

### concrete-abstract | Concrete and abstract
- Abstract words (loneliness, memory) sum up fast but give no picture. Mix in concrete ones so the listener sees first and understands after.

### link | Link to the central idea
- Ask whether the image helps the idea or only decorates. A decorative image may stay, but know what job it has.

## Examples
The lines below were written for teaching, about "someone who will not let go".

- Abstract: "ความทรงจำของเธอยังอยู่กับฉัน ฉันยังเจ็บปวดอยู่ทุกวัน" (her memory is still with me; I still hurt every day)
- Concrete, through the senses: "ฉันยังได้กลิ่นสบู่ของเธอที่ปลอกหมอน / ผ้าเย็นตรงที่เธอเคยนอน" (I can still smell her soap on the pillowcase; the cloth is cool where she used to lie)
- Pretty but general: "ดวงดาวพร่างพรายเหมือนความฝันที่ไม่มีวันจบ" (stars glitter like a dream that never ends) → anyone could write it; it says nothing particular
- Plain and strong: "ฉันยังเทน้ำใส่แก้วสองใบ" (I still pour water into two glasses) → ordinary words, but we see an old habit holding on
- A symbol tied to the idea: a green umbrella behind the door returns in every section in a different state (dry, wet, folded) as the singer's feelings change

## Common Pitfalls
- Reusing pretty stock words (moonlight, breeze, stars) until the images are no longer specific.
- Believing a complicated metaphor is a deep one.
- Mixing several unrelated metaphors in one line.
- Images that do not connect to the idea and are only decoration.
- Avoiding plain language for fear of not sounding "poetic".

## Try It Yourself
(1) Choose one object in your song and write about it for five minutes using at least three senses. (2) Underline one detail that only this song has. (3) Compare it with the image already in the song and ask which says more about the idea. (4) Replace one abstract word with a concrete detail and read it aloud: what was lost, what was gained?
