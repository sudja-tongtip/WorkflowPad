## What It Is
A lyric hook is what the listener remembers after the song ends. It may be a clear idea, a distinctive phrase, one image, or a line that returns with more weight each time.

Berklee's course that starts from the title includes lessons on choosing and developing a title and on developing the title motivically [R1], in line with the idea that a main phrase is a foundation the other sections move toward.

An important limit: with only the lyrics, an AI cannot reliably judge a melodic hook or how catchy the song is when sung. Hook analysis here looks only at the words, the clarity of the idea and its placement.

## Why It Works
- A specific phrase is easier to remember than a general one because the listener sees the picture or understands the situation at once.
- A hook that takes its meaning from the sections before it (the Verses develop, then the hook returns heavier) makes repetition worth more than rote.
- Placement matters: a core idea where the listener expects it (the start or end of a Chorus) is easier to keep.
- Repeating a hook with a small difference each time builds familiarity without boredom.

## How to Use
Start by asking "which line or image should the listener remember", then test whether it is specific, matches the idea of the song, and gets its meaning from what comes before.

### clear-idea | A clear idea
- Test: can you sum up the hook in one sentence? If not, the idea is not clear yet.

### distinctive-phrase | A distinctive phrase
- Look for words that belong to this song and not to others. "ร่มสีเขียวหลังประตู" (the green umbrella behind the door) is easier to remember than "memory".

### verse-chorus | The relation between Verse and Chorus
- Let the Verses prepare the meaning for the hook; when it arrives the listener feels it sum things up or burst out.

### placement | Where the key idea sits
- Try moving the main idea to the start or the end of the section and see which is easier to remember (the result may differ when sung).

### repeat-contrast | Repetition and contrast
- Repeat the hook but change one word, or the context around it, so that each time weighs differently.

## Examples
The lines below were written for teaching.

- A general hook: "ฉันคิดถึงเธอเสมอ" (I always miss you) → clear, but anyone can say it; the listener sees nothing
- A specific hook: "ร่มสีเขียวยังแขวนอยู่หลังประตู" (the green umbrella still hangs behind the door) → a picture, memorable, and it raises a question about why it is still there
- A hook with weight from the Verses: Verse 1 hangs the umbrella up; Verse 2 shows many rainy days and still not picking it up; the Chorus line "the green umbrella still hangs behind the door" then means "I am not ready to let go"
- Repetition with difference: Chorus 1 "ร่มสีเขียวยังแขวนอยู่หลังประตู" / Chorus 3 "ร่มสีเขียวยังแขวนอยู่หลังประตู... แต่ฉันเริ่มมองมันได้" (...but I am starting to be able to look at it) → same words, the meaning moves

## Common Pitfalls
- A clear idea that is too general, so the listener takes nothing specific away.
- Believing more repetition means a better hook when the sections around it do not give the repetition more weight.
- Judging how catchy a hook is from the words alone when it depends on melody and singing too.
- Trying to make every line a hook so that none stands out.
- Not every song needs a hook that is a single striking sentence. A mood piece may have an image as the thing remembered.

## Try It Yourself
(1) After reading your lyrics once, ask a friend (or yourself) which line or image they remember. (2) Write the idea of that line in one sentence. (3) Rewrite the line in two more specific ways and choose. (4) Check whether the Verse before the hook prepares its meaning; if not, add one detail to the Verse. (5) If the hook repeats, change one word in the last repeat and read it aloud. Remember the real effect can only be heard when sung.
