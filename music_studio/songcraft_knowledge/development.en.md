## What It Is
Development is what changes between the start and the end of a song. The listener should feel that they have arrived somewhere that is different from where they began. It does not have to be a big event or a decision by a character; it may be only the meaning of something familiar shifting a little.

Berklee's lyric-writing course that starts from the title includes a lesson called "Verse Development Techniques" [R1], which shows it is a topic of the course. The six approaches below are categories we made for learning.

## Why It Works
- Listeners may stay with a song longer when each section gives something new: a fact, a feeling or a view.
- When the Verses develop, a Chorus that repeats unchanged may weigh more each time, because the listener reads it with what they have just learned.
- Keeping one mood on purpose is also a kind of development, if the small details that change make the stillness mean something.
- The feeling that "the song circles in place" can come when a new section says the old thing in new words and adds nothing. That may be meant in a song that is still on purpose, but ask what the stillness does.

## How to Use
Ask of each section: "what does the listener know or feel differently when it ends?" Choose one of the approaches below for a section you cannot answer for. Keeping a song still on purpose is a valid choice, but ask whether the stillness has been made to mean something.

### event | Event and conflict
- Add something that happens or a new resistance: a new person arrives, something breaks, a hurtful word is said. Suits story songs.

### feeling | A feeling that changes
- Begin with one emotion and let it move, for example from anger to tiredness, from refusing to accepting. It needs details that carry the listener there.

### reveal | New information
- Let the listener learn something they did not know: the real reason, something the character hid. It works best when the new fact makes the earlier section read differently.

### perspective | A change of view
- See the same thing from another person, another time or another distance, for example looking back from the present.

### recontext | A changed meaning of the same text
- A line or Chorus returns in a new context and so means something else. This often gives the feeling that the song closes a circle in a meaningful way.

### stillness | Keeping the mood on purpose
- The main mood does not change, but small details do (a shadow gets longer, tea grows cold), so that time can be felt passing. Ask whether those small changes make the stillness mean something.

## Examples
The lines below were written for teaching, about "a room nobody is in".

- Verse 1 (setting): "ม่านหน้าต่างยังพลิ้วตามลม / ชาในแก้วเย็นไปแล้วครึ่งหนึ่ง" (the curtain still moves in the wind; the tea in the glass has gone half cold)
- A Verse 2 that stands still: "ม่านหน้าต่างยังพลิ้วอยู่เช่นเดิม / ชาในแก้วยังวางอยู่ตรงนั้น" (the curtain still moves as before; the tea still sits there) → the same images again, nothing added
- A Verse 2 with an event: "เช้านี้ฉันเปิดประตูห้องเธอ / ไม่มีใครบอกว่าห้ามเข้าอีกแล้ว" (this morning I opened her door; nobody says I may not come in any more) → something happens
- A Verse 2 with a reveal: "แก้วใบนี้เธอตั้งใจเก็บไว้ให้ฉัน / ฉันเพิ่งรู้จากกระดาษที่แปะใต้ก้น" (she kept this glass for me on purpose; I only found out from the paper stuck under it) → Verse 1 reads differently now
- A Verse 2 that is still on purpose: "เงาของเก้าอี้ยาวขึ้นอีกคืบ / ชาในแก้วมีฝ้าบาง ๆ ลอยอยู่" (the chair's shadow has grown a span longer; a thin film floats on the tea) → same mood, but time visibly passed

Compare: what does the listener know that is new at the end of each? The second gives nothing new, while the last lets us feel time move.

## Common Pitfalls
- Thinking development means a big event or a plot twist.
- Changing the objects or images but not the meaning (new words, same substance).
- Using "it is still on purpose" as a reason without checking what the stillness does.
- Adding an event that has nothing to do with the idea just so that "something happens".
- Forgetting that some songs need thirty seconds of feeling and no long story.
- The techniques in this lesson are options for the writer, not a standard by which a song that does not use them is judged wrong or lesser.

## Try It Yourself
(1) Write one line per section: "after this section the listener knows or feels ___ more." (2) If two sections get the same answer, pick one and rewrite it with one of the six approaches. (3) Read the Chorus after the rewritten section and note whether its meaning changed. (4) If your song is meant to be still, list three small things that change between the start and the end.
