## What It Is
The opening is the first few lines: they tell the listener what world this song is in and where it is about to go. It works like a small promise. The listener starts to expect something, and the rest of the song answers it, changes its meaning, or deliberately overturns it.

Berklee's lyric-writing course that starts from the title teaches choosing a central idea and building the sections around it [R1]. A good opening usually points in that direction, though it does not have to state the idea outright.

The eight patterns below are categories we use for learning. They are not an academic classification and the number is not fixed [R3]. One song can mix several, and some openings fit none.

## Why It Works
- Listeners often decide fairly quickly whether to step into a song. A specific opening may give them an image and a question at once.
- Details that come through the senses (seeing, hearing, touch) may be easier to hold than abstract nouns (Pattison describes sense-bound writing [R2]; the remark about the effect on the listener is ours).
- The opening creates an expectation. A song tends to feel strong when what follows keeps the promise, or changes its meaning for a reason.
- No pattern is best. The right one is the one that helps this song say what it needs to say.

## How to Use
Before choosing, answer three questions about your song: (1) what should the listener see, wonder about or feel first; (2) where in the song will the promise of the opening be answered or changed; (3) which pattern makes this song's idea clearest. Then write the first two lines in several patterns and compare them.

### scene | Scene Opening: open with an image or situation
- Suits: story songs, songs that want the listener to see before feeling.
- Expectation: who is here, where, and what is going on in this scene.
- Strength: concrete, immediate / Limit: a generic image (moon, wind, stars) could open any song and promises nothing.
- After the opening: let the scene matter to the story, for example by returning in the last section with a new meaning.

### declaration | Emotional Declaration: open with a feeling or a statement
- Suits: songs where emotion is the lead, direct songs.
- Expectation: the listener knows the feeling and wants to know why.
- Strength: clear, fast to reach / Limit: naming the feeling before showing a reason can feel flat without details behind it.
- After the opening: use the next section to answer why, with a specific event or image.

### question | Question Opening: open with a question
- Suits: songs where the narrator searches, doubts or is unsure.
- Expectation: the listener wants the answer, or wants to know whose question it is.
- Strength: invites the listener to think along / Limit: a question that is too broad ("why is the world like this") or never answered leaves the expectation hanging.
- After the opening: answer it, change what it means, or leave it open on purpose while making it weigh more each time.

### in-medias-res | In Medias Res: open in the middle of the action
- Suits: songs with events, urgency or tension.
- Expectation: the listener wants to know what came before and how it ends (the term comes from literature [R3]).
- Strength: no time spent setting up, energy from the first line / Limit: with no context at all the listener can get lost.
- After the opening: reveal what led here, in Verse 2 or the pre-chorus.

### dialogue | Dialogue Opening: open with something said
- Suits: songs about a relationship between two people.
- Expectation: who is speaking to whom, and how much the words weigh.
- Strength: a character's voice at once, feels alive / Limit: a line nobody would really say sounds stiff.
- After the opening: let the words return with a new meaning, or give the other person an answer.

### contradiction | Surprise / Contradiction: open with a clash or the unexpected
- Suits: songs with irony, songs that hide a feeling under the surface.
- Expectation: the listener wants to know how the two things can live together.
- Strength: memorable, pulls forward / Limit: with nothing behind it the contradiction is only a trick.
- After the opening: let the rest of the song explain it, or make it hurt more step by step.

### hook-first | Hook / Thesis First: open with the hook or the main idea
- Suits: songs with a short, strong idea; songs that need to be remembered quickly.
- Expectation: the listener knows the core and wants to see how the song develops it.
- Strength: clear and easy to remember / Limit: no secret is left, so the later sections must really develop it.
- After the opening: let the next sections add a fact or a view that makes the idea weigh more.

### atmospheric | Atmospheric / Symbolic: open with a mood or a symbol
- Suits: songs about mood and stillness; songs that use a symbol in place of a feeling.
- Expectation: the listener expects to be inside an atmosphere more than to follow a story.
- Strength: builds a world of its own / Limit: if the mood is the same all the way through with nothing changing, the song can feel stuck.
- After the opening: let the symbol or small details change a little at a time, so that the stillness means something.

## Examples
The lines below were written for teaching and are not from a real song. All of them say the same thing, "someone who is still waiting", but open differently, to show what each pattern promises the listener.

- Scene: "ถ้วยกาแฟสองใบยังวางอยู่ที่เดิม / ฝั่งของเธอยังไม่มีใครแตะ" (two coffee cups are still where they were; nobody has touched her side) → the listener wonders where she is
- Declaration: "ฉันคิดถึงเธอจนไม่รู้จะเริ่มตรงไหน" (I miss you so much I do not know where to start) → the feeling is known, the reason is wanted
- Question: "ถ้าฉันยังรออยู่ที่เดิม เธอจะกลับมาไหม" (if I am still waiting here, will you come back) → the listener waits for the answer
- In medias res: "ฉันกำลังเก็บเสื้อของเธอลงกล่อง มือหยุดตรงแขนเสื้อสีเทา" (I am putting her shirts into a box, my hand stops at the grey sleeve) → what happened before?
- Dialogue: "'เดี๋ยวกลับนะ' เธอว่าแล้วปิดประตู / นั่นคือประโยคสุดท้ายที่เราคุยกัน" ("back soon," she said and closed the door; that was the last thing we said) → one line carries the whole story
- Contradiction: "ฉันยิ้มได้ทุกวัน ตั้งแต่วันที่เธอไม่อยู่" (I can smile every day since the day you were gone) → is the smile real?
- Hook first: "รอเธออยู่ตรงนี้ ที่เดิมทุกวัน" (waiting for you here, the same place every day) → the core is clear; the rest has to make it weigh more
- Atmospheric: "ฝนค้างอยู่บนหลังคาตั้งแต่เมื่อคืน / แสงสีฟ้ายังอยู่ที่ขอบประตู" (rain has stayed on the roof since last night; blue light is still at the edge of the door) → the listener is in the room before knowing the story

Notice that none is "right" or "wrong". Each changes what the listener is waiting for, and the song has to answer it.

## Common Pitfalls
- Opening with a pretty image anyone could write, and then a song that does not continue from it.
- Opening with a question or a puzzle, then never returning to it and not leaving it open on purpose in a way that means something.
- Using contradiction or a twist as a trick with nothing to support it.
- Believing the opening must always be new and unusual. A plain opening is fine if the voice and the idea of the song are clear.
- Using the same pattern in every song because it is the comfortable one.
- The techniques in this lesson are options for the writer, not a standard by which a song that does not use them is judged wrong or lesser.

## Try It Yourself
Take your song (or the one you are writing) and go in order: (1) write what the listener should feel or wonder in the first two lines; (2) rewrite the first two lines in three of the eight patterns; (3) read each aloud and note "what does this promise the listener"; (4) choose the one whose promise best matches the rest of the song, or note which part of the song would have to change to answer that promise. You may well keep the original opening if it already works.
